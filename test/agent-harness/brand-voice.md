and not a production prompt change. Updated 2026-10-02.

## Human Review Rubric

Rate each axis 1-5: 1 conflicts with the baseline, 3 is usable with editing, and
5 is ready on this axis. Record a short reason and an example edit. Any invented
event fact or unsupported health claim blocks editorial approval regardless of
the average score.

| Axis | A strong draft | Warning signs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| NC scene authenticity | Specific, source-supported artists, venues, cities, and musical context | Generic nationwide copy or fabricated local familiarity |
| Community invitation | Welcoming language that helps people connect and participate | Exclusivity, pressure, or treating every show as a sales pitch |
| Musical usefulness | Brief, distinct descriptions that help readers choose a show | Repetitive hype, forced scene slang, invented biography or billing |
| Factual discipline | Dates, location, lineup, ticket links, and access details trace to supplied sources | Invented prices, free entry, set times, sold-out status, or testimonials |
| Mental-health sensitivity | Community and belonging expressed naturally when relevant | Promising treatment, guaranteed health outcomes, or making every concert a wellness claim |
| Mobile readability | Scannable schedule, concise highlights, one useful invitation | Long filler introductions or burying practical details |

Embrace warmth, local specificity, gratitude, curiosity, and natural invitations.
Avoid corporate superlatives, fake firsthand attendance, exaggerated certainty,
forced slang, and claims of therapeutic benefit without an appropriate basis.
Community purpose should guide the writing, not become a mandatory slogan in
every event entry.

Use published newsletters for voice inspiration, not factual ground truth for a
new edition. Synthetic catalog fixtures own the offline facts; current approved
catalog records and verified external information own live edition facts. Human
approval is still required before a newsletter is sent or published.
